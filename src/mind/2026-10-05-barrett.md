**The half-lesson: Your Brain Is Not For Thinking**

Myth: The human brain's primary function is thinking through things logically to weigh options / come up with solutions / etc.

More accurate: The brain's primary objective is resource allocation / management. *Allostasis* -- thought processes, the experience of emotions, etc. are in service of this?

**Lesson 1: You have One Brain ( Not Three )**

Barrett discusses the myth of the [triune brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triune_brain), formalized in the mid-twentieth century by Paul MacLean, who was working with the tools we had at the time, visual inspection. He saw parts in human brains that appeared to match parts of lizard brains, parts of mouse brains, etc. and determined that the parts that didn't match in appearance were unique to our species. Now, we have the ability to see neurons and can see that they're genetically similar across species. The difference appears to be the time allocated to the development of each "part" of the brain (revisit this). With mouse instructions, x amount of time is spent on Y formation and this makes mouse brain. In humans, x-times-whatever-multiplier is spent on Y formation, which leads to new parts of brain growth and the function of neurons is re-allocated to different parts and you have a more complex human brain. Note that the idea that humans have a huge neocortex is false. Proportionally, considering time allocated to its development, it's normal / on par with other animals. 

Ex. there are common brain-manufacturing plans and they are all the same and they run processes. For different animals, each part of the manufacturing process runs for a specific amount of time and this is what produces different creature brains. ???

The misunderstandings described led to the misnaming of the neocortex in humans (it's not new) and to the misconception that humans have a three-part brain: "lizard brain" (survival) / limbic system (emotion) and neocortex (reason). 

The Triune Brain Myth persists in our laws and our anecdotal understanding of human behavior. Ex. someone experiencing extreme emotion not being responsible for their actions according to a criminal court, self-help processes for wrangling "lizard brain" so rational brain wins, etc. More example uses (some in the text some from external sources):

* **Therapy**
  * **Psychoeducation:** Explaining why the thinking mind shuts down during panic
  * **Regulation:** Calming physical survival reflexes before trying talk therapy
  * **Self-Compassion:** Destigmatizing involuntary fear responses like freezing

* **Law**
  * **Culpability:** Explaining impulsive "heat of passion" crimes
  * **Testimony:** Normalizing fragmented memories in trauma survivors
  * **Sentencing:** Advocating for juveniles based on incomplete brain development

* **Business**
  * **Neuromarketing:** Targeting survival instincts to trigger impulse buys
  * **Leadership:** Managing workplace panic during corporate crises
  * **Negotiation:** Keeping clients calm so they can think logically

* **Ethics**
  * **Tribalism:** Showing how fear-mongering turns off empathy
  * **Biases:** Tracking how gut reactions overpower rational ethics
